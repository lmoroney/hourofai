## LinkedIn post
For most people, chatbots are the face of AI. So for my second free Hour of AI lab, I wanted students to see what's actually going on behind that chat box.

"How Chatbots Really Work" is a Google Colab notebook for students aged roughly 13-18, with no ML background needed. In 45-60 minutes they:

• see how text is chopped into tokens (and why chatbots struggle to count the r's in "strawberry")
• look inside a real open language model at its next-word probabilities
• turn the "temperature" up and down and watch the writing go from dull to nonsense
• discover the hidden script that turns a word predictor into a chatbot
• and catch it going wrong: a detailed biography of a scientist I made up, fake sources, contradictory answers, and gender stereotypes it absorbed from its training text

The big idea is simple: a chatbot is a next-word predictor. It learned what text usually looks like, not what's true. Once students get that, "always verify" stops being a rule and becomes common sense.

It runs on free Colab CPU with a small open model: no API keys, no accounts beyond Google, nothing to pay for. There's a teacher guide with a timing plan, discussion questions, and tips for using chatbots wisely in school.

It pairs with my first lab, "Your First AI Model in an Hour" (https://colab.research.google.com/github/lmoroney/hourofai/blob/main/lab1-first-model-in-an-hour/first_model_in_an_hour.ipynb), but works on its own too.

Free for every classroom. If you try it, I'd love to hear how it goes.
